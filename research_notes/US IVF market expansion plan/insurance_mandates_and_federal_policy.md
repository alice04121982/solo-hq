## 1. Which states mandate fertility / IVF coverage, and on what terms?

### Takeaway
Roughly 15 states (plus DC via its benchmark plan) now require state-regulated plans to cover IVF, with ~10 more requiring some lesser infertility or fertility-preservation coverage; California (SB 729) and Minnesota took effect 1 Jan 2026, and Virginia has enacted an IVF benchmark mandate from 2028. Mandates vary widely (cover vs offer, HMO-only, group-size floors, retrieval caps, dollar caps, age limits), and aggregator sites conflict on counts and details.

### Cited Findings

**Headline counts (sources disagree slightly):**
- "As of 2026, 15 states plus Washington, D.C. require insurance plans to cover IVF specifically. Another 10 states mandate some fertility coverage that may not include IVF." — [Nava Benefits 2026 guide](https://www.navabenefits.com/resources/fertility-benefits-by-state-what-insurance-covers-ivf-and-where-coverage-is-mandated) (via search summary)
- IVF-mandate state list given by one 2026 aggregator: Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Hawaii, Illinois, Maryland, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Rhode Island, West Virginia; and "25 states and Washington, D.C. have state ART laws requiring private insurance." — [Carrot / aggregator results incl. doesinsurancecover.com, conceiveguide.com](https://www.doesinsurancecover.com/blog/ivf-insurance-coverage-by-state). **Caution:** West Virginia's law is widely described elsewhere as an HMO-only infertility requirement without IVF ([search summary citing ReproductiveFacts/other](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/patient-advocacy/state-and-territory-infertility-insurance-laws/)); Minnesota and Utah (below) are missing from this list. Treat this list as unreliable.
- A Fertility & Sterility / NCSL-derived count: "21 states require some form of fertility coverage, 20 states have passed laws that require coverage for an infertility diagnosis and/or fertility treatment, and seven states have fertility preservation laws" — this is an **older (2020-era) count**, superseded — [Fertility and Sterility](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(20)30791-3/fulltext)
- ASRM maintains a "State and Territory Infertility Insurance Laws" page — [ReproductiveFacts.org](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/patient-advocacy/state-and-territory-infertility-insurance-laws/)
- "Over half of U.S. states introduced or carried over legislation related to state fertility coverage mandates" in 2026, focusing on commercial insurance rather than Medicaid "due to budget constraints." — [MultiState, 1 Apr 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/1/state-fertility-coverage-mandates-expand-in-2026-legislative-sessions-ivf-and-assisted-reproductive-technology-laws)

**Mandate-to-offer and HMO-only states:**
- "Five states require the offer of coverage: California, Connecticut, Ohio, Texas, and West Virginia" — this is from an **older (c. 2005) Connecticut OLR report**; California and Connecticut have since moved to mandate-to-cover — [CT OLR 2005-R-0236](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2005/rpt/2005-R-0236.htm)
- Texas requires insurers to *offer* IVF benefits, with strict eligibility (marriage requirement, couple's own gametes) — [search summary incl. ReproductiveFacts](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/patient-advocacy/state-and-territory-infertility-insurance-laws/)
- Ohio: HMOs offering basic health services must cover medically necessary infertility, but IVF, GIFT, ZIFT excluded; West Virginia: HMOs must cover infertility, term undefined; Montana: HMOs must cover infertility as a basic service; "Montana, Ohio, and West Virginia require infertility or IVF coverage only inside HMO plans" — [same search summary](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/patient-advocacy/state-and-territory-infertility-insurance-laws/)

## 2. ERISA self-funded exemption, and small-group vs large-group thresholds

### Takeaway
State mandates don't reach self-funded ERISA plans. That is **about two-thirds (67%) of covered workers**, and 80% at firms with 200+ workers. Most new mandates (CA, MN, NY) also apply only to large-group fully insured plans, so the population actually protected by a state IVF mandate is a minority even in mandate states.

### Cited Findings
- KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey: "67% of covered workers, including 27% of covered workers at firms with 10 to 199 workers and 80% at larger firms, are enrolled in plans that are self-funded." — [KFF 2025 EHBS](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/); [Summary PDF](https://files.kff.org/attachment/Employer-Health-Benefits-Survey-2025-Annual-Survey-Summary-of-Findings.pdf)
- KFF 2025: 37% of covered workers at firms with 10-199 workers are in a **level-funded** plan (a small self-funded component plus stop-loss), similar to 2024. Level-funded plans are generally treated as self-funded and therefore escape state mandates. — [KFF 2025 EHBS](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/)
- "Self-funded employer plans (ERISA) are not subject to state mandates regardless of where you live." — [aggregator/Carrot summary](https://www.get-carrot.com/blog/does-insurance-cover-ivf)
- California SB 729 applies only to fully insured large groups (101+), not small group, self-funded or religious employers. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoia.com/2025/07/california-mandates-infertility-ivf-coverage-updated/)
- Group-size floors reported in state mandates: Illinois 25+; New Jersey 50+; New York 100+; California 101+. — sources in Section 1 table.

### Inferences
- A "what coverage do I have?" flow should ask first, "Is your employer plan self-funded (ASO), level-funded, or fully insured?" Most users won't know. HR or the SBC/SPD ("administered by" vs "insured by") is the practical way to find out.
- Small-group and individual (ACA marketplace) coverage of IVF depends on the state's **EHB benchmark plan** (e.g., DC 2026, Virginia 2028), not on group mandates.

### Gaps
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Under the ACA, small group generally means 1-50 employees; CA, CO, NY and VT define it as 1-100. Not re-confirmed this session.
- The share of the population covered by a state IVF mandate wasn't found. KFF and others have estimated it in the past; no current figure retrieved.

## 3. Federal policy (Trump administration, ACA EHB, TRICARE, FEHB, 2026)

### Takeaway
There's no federal IVF mandate. The administration's actions are: EO 14216 (Feb 2025); an Oct 16, 2025 package (EMD Serono drug discounts via TrumpRx, FDA priority review for Pergoveris, and Oct 2025 tri-agency FAQs on standalone fertility benefits); TrumpRx's launch in Feb 2026; and a **May 2026 proposed rule** creating a new "excepted fertility benefit" category, targeted for plan years from 1 Jan 2027. TRICARE IVF expansion was dropped from the FY26 NDAA. FEHB has expanded IVF for 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Executive Order 14216 (18 Feb 2025):**
- EO 14216, "Expanding Access to In Vitro Fertilization," signed 18 Feb 2025, directed the Domestic Policy Assistant to submit policy recommendations within 90 days (≈20 May 2025) on protecting IVF access and reducing out-of-pocket and plan costs. The recommendations weren't publicly released as a standalone document. — [GovInfo DCPD-202500278](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/DCPD-202500278); [Leech Tishman](https://www.leechtishman.com/insights/blog/expanding-access-to-in-vitro-fertilization/); [Prelude, updated May 2026](https://www.preludefertility.com/blog/trumps-ivf-executive-order-what-you-need-to-know)

**Oct 16, 2025 package:**
- Deal with EMD Serono to discount Gonal-f, Ovidrel and Cetrotide via TrumpRx, reported as reducing patient cost "by more than 70%." Pergoveris gets FDA priority review. — [STAT, 16 Oct 2025](https://www.statnews.com/2025/10/16/trump-ivf-plan-increase-access-fertility-treatment/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/16/politics/trump-ivf-emd-serono-trumprx-drug-price); [US News/HealthDay](https://www.usnews.com/news/health-news/articles/2025-10-20/trump-announces-lower-cost-fertility-drug-deal-with-emd-serono)
- The administration issued guidance to encourage employers to offer **standalone fertility benefits "similar to dental and vision"**, with "no federal mandate or subsidies." — [same sources](https://www.statnews.com/2025/10/16/trump-ivf-plan-increase-access-fertility-treatment/); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/benefits-compensation/administration-change-employers-fertility-benefits)
- **ACA FAQs Part 72** (DOL/HHS/Treasury, 16 Oct 2025) explain how fertility benefits can be offered as *independent, noncoordinated excepted benefits*. They must be under a separate insurance policy, with no coordination with the major medical plan's exclusions, and paid per event regardless of other coverage. Because a separate insurance policy is required, **this route can't be self-insured**. — [DOL FAQ Part 72](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/faqs/aca-part-72); [NFP](https://www.nfp.com/insights/new-guidance-on-fertility-benefits/); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/agency-faqs-part-72-clarify-how-fertility-benefits-may-be-offered-as-excepted-benefits/); [WTW Nov 2025](https://www.wtwco.com/en-us/insights/2025/11/departments-issue-faqs-on-fertility-benefits)

**TrumpRx (launched Feb 2026):**
- TrumpRx.gov launched in February 2026. — [White House release, Feb 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/02/president-trump-launches-trumprx-gov-delivering-massive-immediate-savings-to-millions-of-americans); [WH fact sheet](https://whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/02/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-launches-trumprx-gov-to-bring-lower-drug-prices-to-american-patients)
- Reported prices: Gonal-f as low as $168 per pen depending on dose; up to 84% cash-price reduction for Gonal-f, Cetrotide and Ovidrel. Clinics estimate self-pay medication at $1,500-$3,500 per cycle with all three drugs, versus a historical $4,000-$6,000 (before, the three drugs "typically cost $5,000 per IVF cycle"). Only these three EMD Serono drugs are on TrumpRx. — [Fertility Centers of New England](https://www.fertilitycenter.com/fertility_cares_blog/trumprx-for-fertility-medication/); [IVFPharmacy 2026](https://www.ivfpharmacy.com/blog/trumprx-ivf-medications-2026/) (clinic/pharmacy blogs, so moderate reliability)

**May 2026 proposed rule, "Excepted Fertility Benefits":**
- Announced 10 May 2026 by DOL, HHS and Treasury; published in the Federal Register 13 May 2026 (doc 2026-09479). It creates a **new category of limited excepted benefits** for fertility, exempt from ACA annual/lifetime limits and preventive rules, HIPAA portability and MHPAEA. — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/13/2026-09479/excepted-fertility-benefits); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mvib/proposed-rule-would-bring-flexibility-to-employer-fertility-benefits/); [WTW May 2026](https://www.wtwco.com/en-us/insights/2026/05/proposed-rule-to-classify-fertility-benefits-as-a-new-category-of-excepted-benefits)
- Lifetime cap: **$120,000 per participant (together with eligible beneficiaries)**, indexed for plan years after 31 Dec 2027. **Self-insured versions are allowed** if offered only to employees who have also been offered traditional major medical coverage and may decline it. — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mvib/proposed-rule-would-bring-flexibility-to-employer-fertility-benefits/); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/newsletters/tax/2026/hot-topics/may-26/proposed-rules-exempt-fertility-benefits-from-market-requirements)
- Comments were due **13 July 2026**. If finalized, it would apply to plan years beginning on or after **1 Jan 2027**. — [same](https://www.foley.com/p/102mvib/proposed-rule-would-bring-flexibility-to-employer-fertility-benefits/)
- Epstein Becker Green flags "key questions remain." — [EBG](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/proposed-rules-may-expand-access-to-fertility-benefits-but-key-questions-remain)

**TRICARE / military:**
- TRICARE covers IVF only where infertility results from "a serious or severe illness or injury while on active duty." House (Rep. Sara Jacobs) and Senate (Sen. Duckworth) committee provisions to cover ART/IVF for all active-duty members and dependents were **removed from the final FY2026 NDAA** (Dec 2025). — [News From The States](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/no-expansion-military-ivf-coverage-included-final-defense-policy-bill); [19th News](https://19thnews.org/2025/12/2026-ndaa-defense-bill-military-spending/); [Rep. Jacobs, 15 Jul 2025](https://sarajacobs.house.gov/news/press-releases/rep-sara-jacobs-secures-ivf-coverage-expansion-in-committee-passed-ndaa)

**FEHB (federal employees):**
- 2026 plan year: two nationwide plans (**BCBS Standard, GEHA High**) plus many HMOs cover IVF. OPM required HMOs in states with IVF laws to meet those state requirements, and required all carriers to cover fertility preservation for iatrogenic infertility. All carriers must cover IVF drugs for 3 cycles per year. FEHB has "the highest number of plans offering IVF coverage in its history." — [FedTools (updated May 2026)](https://www.fedtools.com/blog/fehb-fertility-coverage-2026); [Checkbook](https://help.checkbook.org/article/451-opm-releases-fehb-policy-goals-and-initiatives-for-plan-year-2026)
- OPM issued a 2027 call letter (CL 2026-07, 31 Mar 2026). Members of Congress wrote to OPM on 29 Jan 2026 asking for an FEHB IVF benefit in plan year 2027. — [OPM CL2026-07](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/USOPM/2026/03/31/file_attachments/3601982/CL2026-07%20-%202026%20FEHB%20Call%20Letter.pdf); [Rep. Walkinshaw letter](https://walkinshaw.house.gov/uploadedfiles/1-29-2026_letter_to_opm_re_fehb_ivf_benefit_plan_year_2027.pdf)

**ACA EHB:**
- IVF isn't a federal essential health benefit. Coverage in individual and small-group plans depends on each state's EHB benchmark (e.g., DC adds infertility treatment from 2026; Virginia adds up to 3 IVF cycles from 2028). — [MultiState 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/1/state-fertility-coverage-mandates-expand-in-2026-legislative-sessions-ivf-and-assisted-reproductive-technology-laws); [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/michelle-andrews-on-infertility-coverage/)

### Inferences
- The federal approach works through markets (drug discounts and optional employer add-ons) rather than mandates. A $120k lifetime excepted benefit could become a new product category from 2027 that CairnFertility's tool should recognise ("Do you have a separate fertility benefit card or plan?").
- TrumpRx pricing mainly helps **self-pay** patients. Insured patients' costs depend on their plan's pharmacy benefit.

### Gaps
- Contents of the Oct 2025 CL 2026-08 and the 2027 FEHB call letter on IVF weren't read (fetch blocked).
- Whether the excepted-benefit rule has been finalized as of late Sept 2026: **no final rule found**. Assume still proposed.
- Whether other manufacturers (e.g., Organon, Ferring) joined TrumpRx for fertility drugs: not found.

## 4. Employer fertility benefit prevalence and vendors

### Takeaway
Nearly half of large employers (500+) cover IVF (Mercer: 47% in 2024), rising to 70% of employers with 20,000+ workers. Broader all-size surveys (IFEBP) put IVF coverage at about a third of employers. Carve-out vendors (Progyny, Carrot, Maven, Kindbody) administer much of this.

### Cited Findings
- Mercer National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans 2024: **47% of large employers covered IVF in 2024** (45% in 2023), and **70% of employers with 20,000+ employees** (up from 62%). **64%** of IVF-covering employers extend it to people who don't meet the clinical infertility definition. Elective egg freezing is covered by 21% and elective sperm freezing by 20% of large employers. — [Mercer release](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/about/newsroom/employers-enhanced-health-benefits-in-2024-adding-coverage-for-weight-loss-medications-and-ivf-despite-growing-health-cost/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241120305316/en/employers-enhanced-health-benefits-in-2024-adding-coverage-for-weight-loss-medications-and-ivf-despite-growing-health-costs-mercer-survey-finds)
- IFEBP (fall 2024): **42%** of US employers offer fertility benefits (40% in 2022, 30% in 2020). **32%** offer IVF coverage (13% in 2016, 30% in 2022). **16%** offer egg harvesting/freezing (2% in 2016). — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/fertility-benefit-on-the-rise/725390/); [IFEBP blog](https://blog.ifebp.org/fertility-benefits-rapidly-rising-as-employers-look-to-attract-and-support-talent/)
- Vendor landscape comparison (Progyny, Kindbody, Carrot, Maven), with fertility benefit coverage "increasing 33% in two years." — [Fertility Bridge](https://www.fertilitybridge.com/news-articles/fertility-benefit-coverage-progyny-kindbody-carrot-maven)
- Mercer guidance on delivering efficient IVF coverage — [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/us-health-news/delivering-efficient-and-affordable-ivf-coverage-to-employees/)

### Inferences
- Because most large employers self-fund, **employer choice (often via a vendor such as Progyny or Carrot) matters more than state law for most insured Americans.** The tool should ask about the vendor ("Do you have Progyny / Carrot / Maven / Kindbody?").

### Gaps
- Mercer 2025 figures (published ~Nov 2025) weren't retrieved. KFF 2025 EHBS fertility-specific figures weren't retrieved.
- Vendor market shares and member counts weren't retrieved.

## 5. State Medicaid coverage of fertility care

### Takeaway
Medicaid almost never covers IVF. Only New York, Utah (narrow, genetic-carrier IVF) and DC offer any infertility-treatment coverage, and a handful of states cover fertility preservation for iatrogenic infertility.

### Cited Findings
- "New York, Utah, and Washington, D.C. are the only states that offer Medicaid coverage for infertility treatments." In NY and DC this is limited to **3 cycles of ovulation-inducing medications**. **Utah** adopted a 2024 state plan amendment covering **IVF and genetic testing only for carriers of certain genetic diseases**. — [Nava Benefits / search summary](https://www.navabenefits.com/resources/fertility-benefits-by-state-what-insurance-covers-ivf-and-where-coverage-is-mandated); [RESOLVE Medicaid page](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/insurance-coverage/medicaid-coverage-for-infertility-treatments-and-fertility-preservation/)

## 6. Tax angles (HSA/FSA, medical expense deduction, state credits)

### Takeaway
IVF (including egg and sperm storage) is a qualified medical expense. It's HSA/FSA-eligible and deductible above 7.5% of AGI if you itemise. Gestational-surrogate costs aren't eligible. A 2025 IRS private letter ruling allowed IVF costs for a same-sex male couple's own genetic material/IVF portion but not the surrogacy costs.

### Cited Findings
- IRS Publication 502 (2025) lists IVF, "including temporary storage of eggs or sperm," as a deductible medical expense. Amounts paid for "identification, retention, compensation, and medical care of a gestational surrogate" are excluded. — [IRS Pub 502](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502)
- Itemised medical expenses are deductible above **7.5% of AGI**. HSA/FSA-reimbursed amounts can't be double-counted. — [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/conditions/fertility/ivf-tax-deductible); [IRS Pub 502](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502)
- IRS PLR 202505002 (released 31 Jan 2025) addresses IVF-related expenses. The Tax Adviser (Nov 2025) summarises it: "IRS approves medical deduction for IVF, denies it for surrogacy." — [IRS PLR 202505002](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/202505002.pdf); [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2025/nov/irs-approves-medical-deduction-for-ivf-denies-it-for-surrogacy/)
- Surrogacy expenses aren't HSA/FSA-eligible. — [buyFSA](https://buyfsa.com/blogs/fsa-hsa-eligibility/are-surrogate-expenses-fsa-hsa-eligible)

### Inferences
- In the UK framing, the "tax relief" message is modest. In practice only high-cost, lower-AGI patients benefit from itemising, while HSA/FSA use is the more common lever.

### Gaps
- **State tax credits for IVF:** none found this session. No state IVF tax credit was confirmed. 2026 HSA/FSA contribution limits weren't retrieved.
- A "$15,000 IVF tax deduction" claim appears on a clinic blog ([Life IVF](https://lifeivfcenter.com/blog/ivf-tax-deduction/)). It's unclear and probably conflated with something else. **Don't use it** without verification.
