# US Legal and Regulatory Landscape for IVF and Family-Building (as of September 2026)

Method note: research done 26 Sept 2026 via web search. Many primary sites (fda.gov, congress.gov, asrm.org, lgbtmap.org, nclrights.org, multistate.us) were blocked for full-page fetch in this environment, so several findings rest on search-result snippets of those pages rather than full reads. Where a claim comes from a commercial agency or low-authority aggregator, that is flagged. Anything not sourced is in Gaps.

## 1. Federal regulation of ART (FDA, CDC, CLIA, no HFEA-equivalent, professional self-regulation)

### Takeaway
There is no US licensing body equivalent to the HFEA: federal oversight is limited to FDA infection-control rules for donated tissue (21 CFR 1271), mandatory-but-thinly-enforced CDC success-rate reporting (1992 Act), and lab rules, with most clinical practice governed by ASRM/SART self-regulation. The FDA's 1990s-era deferral of men who have had sex with men (MSM) from anonymous sperm donation was proposed for removal in January 2025 draft guidance, but I found no evidence it had been finalised by Sept 2026. The CDC's ART surveillance team was eliminated in April 2025, which puts federal success-rate publishing in doubt.

### Cited Findings
**FDA / 21 CFR 1271**
- 21 CFR Part 1271 Subpart C sets donor-eligibility requirements (screening and testing for communicable diseases) for human cells, tissues and cellular and tissue-based products (HCT/Ps), which include donated sperm, eggs and embryos — [eCFR 21 CFR 1271 Subpart C](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-L/part-1271/subpart-C)
- The FDA's standing donor-eligibility guidance (2007) is the source of the MSM deferral. Under that policy, men who had sex with men in the previous 5 years are ineligible as anonymous reproductive tissue donors whatever their test results — [FDA Guidance: Eligibility Determination for Donors of HCT/Ps](https://www.fda.gov/files/vaccines,%20blood%20&%20biologics/published/Eligibility-Determination-for-Donors-of-Human-Cells--Tissues--and-Cellular-and-Tissue-Based-Products--Guidance-for-Industry.pdf); [Buckeye Flame, Jan 2025](https://thebuckeyeflame.com/2025/01/16/queer-men-will-finally-be-able-to-donate-their-tissue/)
- On 6–7 Jan 2025 the FDA published draft guidance, "Recommendations for Determining Eligibility of Donors of HCT/Ps". It proposes the same individual risk-based HIV/HBV/HCV questions for every donor regardless of sex or gender, replacing the time-based MSM deferral. Comments closed 6 Feb 2025 — [Federal Register 2025-01-07, doc 2024-31524](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/07/2024-31524/recommendations-for-determining-eligibility-of-donors-of-human-cells-tissues-and-cellular-and); [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-01-07/pdf/2024-31524.pdf)
- The draft is not yet in force: "the tissue donor regulations have not officially been updated" — [Buckeye Flame](https://thebuckeyeflame.com/2025/01/16/queer-men-will-finally-be-able-to-donate-their-tissue/). For comparison, the equivalent blood-donation policy was finalised and took effect in 2023 — [Blood Assurance](https://www.bloodassurance.org/blood-donation-education/about-blood-donations/fda-donor-deferral-updates/)
- The FDA enforces Part 1271 against fertility clinics through warning letters. Examples: Invia Fertility Specialists PLLC (13 Jun 2025), Conceive Fertility Center (27 Jan 2026) and Central Coast Multispecialty Medical Group (29 Jan 2026) — [FDA warning letter, Invia](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/invia-fertility-specialists-pllc-710632-06132025); [Conceive Fertility Center](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/conceive-fertility-center-722459-01272026); [Central Coast](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/central-coast-multispecialty-medical-group-inc-722521-01292026) (letter contents not read, because the fetch was blocked)
- New York adds its own state layer: NY State Department of Health tissue-bank licensing on top of FDA rules for reproductive tissue — [Mens Reproductive Health](https://mensreproductivehealth.com/navigating-fda-guidelines-and-ny-state-regulations-on-cryopreserved-male-reproductive-tissue/)

**CDC reporting (Fertility Clinic Success Rate and Certification Act 1992)**
- ART programmes must report outcomes under the 1992 federal statute, and the CDC must publish them — [Healthcare Brew, Apr 2025](https://www.healthcare-brew.com/stories/2025/04/11/hhs-layoffs-reproductive-health-research)
- In the April 2025 HHS reductions in force, the CDC's entire six-person ART surveillance team was eliminated and some work was paused "indefinitely". Clinics can still submit data through the Westat-run web system, whose CDC contract runs to 14 Oct 2027, but there is no clear route for analysing and publishing it — [Healthcare Brew](https://www.healthcare-brew.com/stories/2025/04/11/hhs-layoffs-reproductive-health-research); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/cdcs-ivf-team-gutted-even-trump-calls-fertilization-president-rcna199261); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbestv/2025/04/03/hhs-job-cuts-entire-cdc-team-focused-on-infertility-and-ivf-is-gone/); [Fertility Bridge](https://www.fertilitybridge.com/news-articles/cdc-fires-ivf-arts-team-data-vacuum)
- A STAT op-ed by a fertility doctor (June 2025) argued that IVF needs more regulation after the cuts — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/06/16/ivf-regulation-cdc-hhs-art-surveillance-fertility-clinic-data-success-rates/)

**Self-regulation (ASRM/SART)**
- SART membership requires compliance with its quality assurance, validation, advertising and embryo-transfer guidelines. Members must do at least 20 aspirations/transfers a year, and new practices need a board-certified reproductive endocrinologist (REI) as medical director — [SART: Join SART](https://www.sart.org/professionals-and-providers/join-sart/)
- SART runs its own outcome registry and clinic summary reports (CSRs) at sart.org, and clinics' "predictor" pages sit on sartcorsonline.com — [SART clinic data](https://sartcorsonline.com/Predictor/ClinicLanding)
- A 2025 Fertility & Sterility abstract compares success rates at SART-member and non-member clinics, so non-SART clinics do exist and are outside this self-regulation — [Fertil Steril 2025](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(25)00365-6/fulltext)

### Inferences
- For UK readers the key message is that the US has no HFEA: no statutory licence to practise IVF, no national donor register, no federal cap on donor offspring, and no statutory embryo-storage limit. Quality signals are voluntary (SART membership, CAP/ASRM lab accreditation), plus state law.
- With CDC publishing in limbo, SART's CSR is likely to be the only practical national source of clinic-level success data. The site should link to SART CSRs and date-stamp all figures.
- Until the FDA finalises the 2025 draft, gay and bisexual men can still generally use a **directed/known** donor pathway (FDA rules allow ineligible directed donors with labelling and consent). Anonymous bank donation stays blocked for MSM active within 5 years. Two dads using a friend's sperm, or one partner's, is not blocked. The directed-donor exception is based on my knowledge of 1271.65 and was not verified in this pass.

### Gaps
- Whether the Jan 2025 HCT/P donor-eligibility draft guidance was finalised by Sept 2026. No source found, so assume still draft.
- Whether CDC published any ART report (for 2022/2023 data) after April 2025, and whether the posts were restored. Not found.
- CLIA's role: ART embryology labs are not clearly covered by CLIA (andrology/endocrine testing is). CAP/ASRM reproductive lab accreditation is voluntary. I had no fetched source for this, so it needs verifying.
- Current ASRM embryo-transfer number guidance (latest ASRM/SART committee opinion). Not fetched. The widely reported position is single embryo transfer for euploid embryos/favourable prognosis.

## 2. Embryo personhood and post-Dobbs risk; state and federal IVF-protection laws

### Takeaway
In LePage (Feb 2024) the Alabama Supreme Court held that frozen embryos are "children" under the state's Wrongful Death of a Minor Act. Alabama responded with a clinic-immunity law (SB159, signed 6 Mar 2024) rather than overturning the personhood reasoning. Since then, a wave of "right to IVF" statutes has passed, several of them in Republican states (Tennessee, Georgia, Colorado in 2025; Tennessee, Georgia, Louisiana, Nevada reported in 2026), and personhood proposals continue (e.g. Montana). Federal Right to IVF bills (2026 versions in House and Senate) remain unenacted. The Trump administration's Oct 2025 action focused on drug prices and employer benefits, not legal protection.

### Cited Findings
- LePage v. Center for Reproductive Medicine (Ala. 2024): the court held that frozen embryos are minor children for the purposes of the Wrongful Death of a Minor Act, exposing clinics to liability for accidental embryo loss — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LePage_v._Center_for_Reproductive_Medicine); [Federalist Society](https://fedsoc.org/commentary/fedsoc-blog/lepage-v-center-for-reproductive-medicine-p-c); [Milbank Memorial Fund](https://www.milbank.org/quarterly/opinions/challenges-for-in-vitro-fertilization-after-alabamas-decision-in-lepage-v-center-for-reproductive-medicine/)
- Alabama SB159, signed by Gov. Ivey on 6 March 2024, gives IVF providers civil and criminal immunity for "death or damage to an embryo". It extends to manufacturers of IVF goods and embryo transporters, and it is retroactive to the LePage ruling. Clinics resumed services afterwards. Critics note that it leaves patients whose embryos are destroyed with no remedy, and it does not change the embryo-as-child holding — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ivf-caught-crosshairs-aftermath-lepage-decision); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppardhealthlaw.com/2024/03/articles/healthcare/ivf-caught-in-the-crosshairs-the-aftermath-of-the-lepage-decision/); [Epstein Becker Green](https://healthlawadvisor.com/update-alabama-legislature-moves-to-shield-ivf-from-personhood-ruling)
- 2025 state IVF-protection laws:
  - Tennessee's Fertility Treatment and Contraceptive Act (SB 449), effective 1 Jul 2025
  - Georgia HB 428, effective 1 Jul 2025, codifies a right to IVF
  - Colorado HB 1259 affirms the right to fertility care
  - Louisiana modernised its IVF statutes with provider protections

- 2026: a search summary of MultiState reports that Tennessee, Georgia, Louisiana and Nevada enacted IVF-protection legislation, and that more than half of states introduced or carried over fertility-mandate bills — [MultiState Apr 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/1/state-fertility-coverage-mandates-expand-in-2026-legislative-sessions-ivf-and-assisted-reproductive-technology-laws) (snippet only; the full page was blocked, so specific 2026 bill numbers are unverified)
- Personhood: a Montana legislator introduced a constitutional personhood referendum proposal ("at any stage of development, beginning at … fertilization") aimed at the 2026 ballot, which needed a two-thirds legislative vote. Personhood bills have also been proposed in Colorado, Georgia and Iowa — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/personhood-bills-ivf-restrictions-alabama-rcna140228); [Montana Independent](https://montanaindependent.substack.com/p/personhood-bill-moves-to-montana); [Cornell JLPP 2025](https://publications.lawschool.cornell.edu/jlpp/2025/03/04/legal-consequences-of-the-fetal-personhood-movement/); [Pregnancy Justice](https://www.pregnancyjusticeus.org/resources/embryonic-personhood-threatens-ivf/)

- Trump administration IVF initiative, 16 Oct 2025, in two parts:

  - DOL/HHS/Treasury guidance that employers may offer fertility benefits as standalone "excepted benefits"

- A proposed rule, "Excepted Fertility Benefits", was published on 13 May 2026 — [Federal Register 2026-09479](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/13/2026-09479/excepted-fertility-benefits); [DOL EBSA release 10 May 2026](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/ebsa/ebsa20260510); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/feds-issue-proposed-rule-fertility-benefit-coverage/819893/); [Health Affairs Forefront](https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/trump-administration-targets-excepted-benefits-fertility-coverage)
- Coverage mandates (these affect access, not legality):

### Inferences
- Alabama remains the reference case: IVF is available there, but the legal theory that embryos are children still stands. A future court or legislature could revive liability, and patients have no remedy for embryo loss. The site should give Alabama patients a specific caution (for example, consider storage or transport arrangements).
- The "right to IVF" statutes in TN, GA and others protect access but typically do not settle embryo status. State-by-state guidance should record (a) an explicit statutory IVF protection, (b) any personhood language in the constitution or code, and (c) any clinic-immunity law.
- Federal protection is unlikely in the near term. The site should not tell users that IVF is federally protected.

## 3. Legal parentage for single, LGBTQ+ and donor-conceived families

### Takeaway
Parentage law is set state by state and varies sharply. Only about a dozen states have adopted UPA 2017 or equivalent modern parentage acts (e.g. CA, CO, CT, ME, MA, RI, VT, WA). These give gender-neutral, marital-status-neutral parentage by consent to assisted reproduction and protect known-donor arrangements. Elsewhere, non-biological parents (especially in two-mum and two-dad families and reciprocal IVF) are commonly advised to get a court order or adoption. Per MAP, 12 states have a statutory confirmatory adoption process, and 26 states plus DC offer second-parent adoption regardless of marital status. Colorado is the only state with a comprehensive donor-conceived persons law (identity release at 18, cap of 25 families, from 2025).

### Cited Findings
- UPA 2017 enactments. A 2026 50-state rollup lists California, Colorado, Connecticut, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Massachusetts, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont and Washington, and says Delaware and Pennsylvania are considering it — [USDC 50-state rollup (2026)](https://usdiagnosticscenter.com/blogs/news/uniform-parentage-act-2017-state-adoption-status-a-50-state-rollup); [ULC Parentage Act page](https://www.uniformlaws.org/committees/community-home?CommunityKey=c4f37d2d-4d20-4be0-8256-22dd73af068f); [NCLR UPA 2017 page](https://www.nclrights.org/our-work/legislation-policy/uniform-parentage-act-of-2017/). CAUTION: the USDC list comes from a low-authority commercial site. Hawaii, Illinois and Oregon in particular should be checked against the ULC enactment map. Illinois' 2015 Parentage Act predates UPA 2017.
- MAP (data updated 11 Sept 2026): 12 states offer a statutory confirmatory adoption process, which confirms an existing parent's legal relationship. 26 states plus DC and 1 territory offer second-parent adoption regardless of marital status. All states allow stepparent adoption for married or recognised couples — [MAP Parental Recognition Laws](https://www.lgbtmap.org/equality-maps/second_parent_adoption_laws) (search snippet; state lists not retrieved)
- MAP's 2023 report, "Relationships at Risk", sets out why outdated state parentage laws leave LGBTQ+ and donor-conceived families insecure — [MAP 2023 report](https://www.mapresearch.org/2023-parentage-report); [PDF](https://www.mapresearch.org/file/MAP-June2023-ParentageReport.pdf)
- Colorado Donor-Conceived Persons and Families of Donor-Conceived Persons Protection Act (SB22-224):
  - Effective 1 Jan 2025 (compliance deadline extended by 2024 amendments). Not retroactive.
  - Donors after that date must agree to release their identity to offspring at 18.
  - Limit of 25 families per donor, worldwide. Family-to-family embryo donation is not counted.
  - Responsible entities must collect donor medical history and try to update it every 3 years.

  — [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/state-news/new-colorado-law-takes-away-anonymity-of-sperm-and-egg-donors-starting-in-2025); [USDCC 2024 amendments](https://www.usdcc.org/2024/05/09/colorado-general-assembly-clarifies-language-in-donor-conceived-persons-protection-act-and-extends-compliance-deadline/); [Cofertility](https://www.cofertility.com/family-learn/what-the-new-colorado-law-on-anonymous-egg-donation-means-for-intended-parents)
- A further 2025 Colorado session law touches this area. Its content was not verified — [Colorado 2025a SL 304](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2025A/bills/sl/2025a_sl_304.pdf)
- Michigan's 2024 Act 24 (Assisted Reproduction and Surrogacy Parentage Act) modernised parentage for assisted reproduction and surrogacy, effective April 2025 — [Michigan Legislature, Act 24 of 2024](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-Act-24-of-2024)
- ASRM Ethics Committee (2021): fertility programmes should treat all persons regardless of marital status, sexual orientation or gender identity — [ASRM Ethics Committee opinion 2021](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/ethics-opinions/access-to-fertility-treatment-irrespective-of-marital-status-sexual-orientation-or-gender-identity-an-ethics-committee-opinion-2021/)

### Inferences
- For two-mum families (including reciprocal IVF) and two-dad families, the site's default guidance should be that even married couples are commonly advised to get a confirmatory/second-parent adoption or a court parentage judgment. The reason is that a birth certificate is not a judgment, and interstate recognition of judgments is more secure. This is standard LGBTQ+ legal-advocacy guidance (GLAD/NCLR), but it was not sourced in this pass.
- Known-donor risk is highest in states without modern donor statutes. In those states an at-home insemination with a known donor may leave the donor as a legal parent. A UK comparison matters here: the UK has a statutory framework, while in the US the result depends on the state and on whether a physician was involved.
- Donor anonymity: the US is broadly still a market of anonymous or "open-ID" donation, with no national register and no federal cap. The exception is Colorado, where a 25-family cap and identity release apply to donors recruited after 2025.

- MAP's state-by-state lists (confirmatory adoption states, second-parent adoption states, states with hostile or unclear law). The page was blocked.
- States with known-donor statutes. Not retrieved.
- Donor compensation rules. I found no sourced data. Background: the US has no federal ban on paying gamete donors (unlike the UK compensation cap). ASRM's former egg-donor compensation guidance ($5,000/$10,000) was dropped after the 2016 Kamakana antitrust settlement. Unverified this pass.
- Any other states that passed donor-conceived-person laws in 2025–26 (e.g. proposals in NY or elsewhere). Not found.

## 4. Surrogacy law by state (site plans surrogacy later)

### Takeaway
Since Michigan legalised compensated gestational surrogacy (effective 1 April 2025, per most sources), compensated surrogacy is effectively prohibited only in Nebraska (contracts void) and Louisiana (altruistic only, limited to married heterosexual couples using their own gametes). Most other states either permit it by statute (e.g. CA, WA, CO, IL, NY, NJ, CT, MI) or permit it through case law and court practice. Surrogacy parentage for single and same-sex intended parents is secure in the statutory-permissive states and less predictable in states with no statute.

### Cited Findings
- Michigan's Assisted Reproduction and Surrogacy Parentage Act (Act 24 of 2024):
  - Repeals the criminal and civil bans on surrogacy.
  - Allows compensated and altruistic gestational surrogacy.
  - Provides pre-birth parentage orders, so intended parents go on the birth certificate without adoption.
  - Michigan was the last state with a broad criminal surrogacy ban.

## 5. Discrimination and access (single and same-sex patients, religious refusals, age limits)

### Takeaway
No federal law expressly prohibits fertility clinics from refusing single or LGBTQ+ patients. Protection comes from state public-accommodation and civil-rights laws, which vary. California is the strongest example: in *North Coast Women's Care v. Superior Court* (2008), the state Supreme Court held that religious objection did not excuse refusing a lesbian patient, and the SB 729 coverage mandate (2026) bans discrimination by sexual orientation, gender identity and marital status. ASRM ethics guidance calls for equal access, but it is not binding law. Age limits are clinic policy, not statute.

### Cited Findings
- North Coast Women's Care Medical Group v. Superior Court (Benitez), Cal. Sup. Ct., 18 Aug 2008, unanimous. Doctors who refused IUI to a lesbian patient on religious grounds could not rely on religious freedom to avoid California's Unruh Civil Rights Act — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1291204.html); [Lambda Legal](https://lambdalegal.org/case/benitez-v-north-coast-womens-care-medical-group/); [NCLR press release](https://www.nclrights.org/about-us/press-release/california-supreme-court-unanimously-rules-that-doctors-cannot-deny-treatment-to-lesbian-patients/)
- California SB 729 expressly prohibits discrimination in fertility coverage based on sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status or family structure — [UCLA](https://law.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/PDFs/Center_on_Reproductive_Health/California%E2%80%99s%20SB%20729%20Expanding%20Access%20to%20IVF%20and%20Family-Building%20for%20All%20(1).pdf); [AJOG 2025](https://www.ajog.org/article/S0002-9378(25)00750-1/fulltext); [Clarkson Law](https://clarksonlawfirm.com/ca-expanded-fertility-insurance-coverage-2026/)
- ASRM's 2021 Ethics Committee opinion supports access irrespective of marital status, sexual orientation or gender identity — [ASRM](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/ethics-opinions/access-to-fertility-treatment-irrespective-of-marital-status-sexual-orientation-or-gender-identity-an-ethics-committee-opinion-2021/)

### Inferences
- In states with no sexual-orientation or marital-status public-accommodation protection, and in states with broad religious-refusal or "medical conscience" laws, clinics may lawfully decline single or LGBTQ+ patients. The site could usefully flag clinics that openly welcome solo and LGBTQ+ patients, but only with verified data.
- Older coverage mandates often define infertility heterosexually (e.g. "12 months of unprotected intercourse"), which excludes solo and same-sex patients from coverage. Newer laws (CA SB 729 and others) use inclusive definitions. This matters for cost guidance.

### Gaps
- A state list of public-accommodation laws covering sexual orientation or marital status as they apply to clinics, and of medical-conscience/religious-refusal statutes (e.g. Mississippi, Tennessee, Arkansas, Ohio conscience laws). Not retrieved.
- Statutory age limits. None found; I believe none exist, but this is unverified.

## 6. Consumer protection, advertising and business-model law (FTC, SART, Anti-Kickback Statute, fee-splitting, patient brokering, corporate practice of medicine)

### Takeaway
A comparison site's biggest legal exposures are:
1. Success-rate and health claims, where the FTC requires substantiation and SART policy requires CSR-linked data.
2. Referral-fee or lead-gen revenue. The federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) is mostly out of scope for cash-pay IVF, but OIG says carving out federal business does not immunise an arrangement that has a federal nexus.
3. State patient-brokering and fee-splitting laws such as Florida § 817.505. These apply regardless of payer and criminalise per-referral or per-patient payments.

Flat-fee, non-contingent advertising is the safest model.

### Cited Findings
- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) replaced the 1998 dietary-supplement guide and covers essentially all health-related advertising claims. Claims need "competent and reliable scientific evidence", and anecdotes or testimonials alone are insufficient — [FTC blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2022/12/whats-new-what-isnt-ftcs-just-published-health-products-compliance-guidance); [FTC guidance PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2023/01/ftc-issues-new-guidance-on-health-related-claims-to-replace-the-dietary-supplements-advertising-guide)
- SART advertising guidelines (revised Jan 2018): clinics should link directly to their SART CSR as the main display of success rates. Supplemental rates are allowed only with specified variables and a disclaimer. The guidelines prohibit unsubstantiated, superiority and denigrating claims — [Fertil Steril: SART advertising guidelines](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(20)30625-7/fulltext)
- Compliance is weak. Of 361 SART-member websites, 31.6% published supplemental rates; of those, only 68.4% linked to their CSR, 53.5% used current data and 67.5% included the disclaimer — [Fertil Steril 2020](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(20)32202-0/fulltext); [PMC follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8441565/)
- AKS / OIG: an arrangement that "carves out" federal programme business can still be within the AKS if it has some nexus to federally billable services, and payments tied to non-federal business can disguise remuneration for federal business — [WilmerHale on OIG advisory opinion](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20231025-oig-issues-advisory-opinion-on-federal-anti-kickback-statute); [OIG AO 22-09](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/advisory-opinions/1031/AO-22-09.pdf); [OIG AO 21-14](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/advisory-opinions/1002/AO-21-14.pdf)
- OIG warned in 2025 that flat fees are not automatically immune: fair market value and commercial reasonableness are separate requirements that both apply — [Holland & Knight, July 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/office-of-inspector-general-highlights-flat-fee); [Paul Hastings on AO 25-04/25-08](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/when-customer-requirements-create-kickback-risk-lessons-from-oig-advisory-opinions)
- Marketing generally can amount to technical AKS violations — [U. Miami Business Law Review](https://repository.law.miami.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1183&context=umblr); [Holland & Hart](https://www.hollandhart.com/pdf/MarketingTrapsforHealthcareProviders.pdf)
- Florida Patient Brokering Act, Fla. Stat. § 817.505:
  - Unlawful for any person to offer or pay any commission, bonus, rebate, kickback, bribe or split-fee to induce patient referrals, or to solicit or receive such payments, including for the acceptance or acknowledgment of treatment.
  - Applies to any health care provider or facility, regardless of payer. It is a criminal statute.

  — [Fla. Stat. § 817.505 (2025)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/817.505); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xlvi-crimes/fl-st-sect-817-505/); [Gassman Law chapter](https://gassmanlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Chapter_6.pdf)

### Inferences
- Most IVF in the US is cash or commercial-insurance pay, so the federal AKS will rarely bite directly. Exceptions are clinics treating TRICARE, Medicaid or FEHB patients, and bundled federal-reimbursable services such as diagnostics.
- State laws are the real constraint: patient brokering (FL), fee-splitting rules in state medical practice acts, and all-payer anti-kickback statutes in several states (e.g. CA B&P Code § 650, TX, MA; unverified this pass).
- Safest monetisation for CairnFertility in the US: fixed-fee listings or advertising not tied to referrals, bookings or conversions, set at fair market value, with clear "sponsored" labelling. That labelling also satisfies the FTC Endorsement Guides (not researched here).
- Pay-per-lead or per-booking commissions from clinics carry high risk in Florida and other all-payer states.
- Corporate practice of medicine (CPOM) matters only if the site employs clinicians or directs clinical care. It applies in CA, NY, TX and others, and MSO structures are used there (unverified).
- Displaying success rates: the safest approach is to show SART CSR data verbatim, with dataset year and link. Comparing clinics by rates carries FTC deception risk if the comparison is misleading (patient-mix differences).

## 7. Embryo disposition and storage law; clinic failures and litigation

### Takeaway
No federal law governs embryo storage periods or disposition. Disputes are resolved by clinic consent contracts and state case law. Notable failures:
- **March 2018:** simultaneous tank failures at Pacific Fertility Center (San Francisco), with about 2,500 eggs and 1,500 embryos affected, and University Hospitals (Cleveland).
- **2021:** a $15m jury verdict that put 90% of fault on the tank maker.
- **2025–26:** ongoing embryo mix-up suits.

Alabama's SB159 immunity now bars such claims against Alabama providers.

### Cited Findings
- Pacific Fertility Center 2018 tank failure: about 2,500 eggs and 1,500 embryos destroyed, 176 related cases. In June 2021 a federal jury in San Francisco awarded about $15m (over $14m for emotional distress) to three women and a couple. It found a manufacturing defect and held the tank manufacturer (Chart Industries) 90% responsible for failing to recall the part — [Enjuris](https://www.enjuris.com/medical-malpractice/ivf-medical-malpractice-lawsuits/); [Lieff Cabraser](https://www.lieffcabraser.com/fertility-law/); [Contemporary OB/GYN](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/ivf-storage-tank-failures-spark-reviews-lawsuits)
- 2025 embryo mix-up (Florida): a couple sued IVF Life and Dr. Milton McNichol after the birth of a child not genetically related to them. The error may date from storage years earlier or from the 2025 transfer — [Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/florida-couple-sues-fertility-clinic-after-ivf-embryo-mix-up)
- NBC (2025) reported that US patients harmed by IVF errors have limited legal protections, with no national incident reporting like the HFEA's — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/womens-health/ivf-errors-legal-protections-nightmare-mistakes-lawsuits-rcna194215)
- Plaintiff firms are actively investigating IVF storage and negligence cases in 2025–26 — [Sauder Schelkopf](https://sauderschelkopf.com/investigations/ivf-errors-embryo-storage-failures-and-fertility-negligence-lawsuit-investigations/)

### Inferences
- UK readers will be surprised that there is no statutory storage limit (the UK has a 10-year renewable limit) and no regulator incident reporting. The site should advise US users to read storage, disposition and divorce/death clauses in clinic consent forms and to confirm storage fees.
- In Alabama, SB159 immunity means patients effectively bear the risk of embryo loss.
- Louisiana (La. R.S. 9:121–133, from my knowledge, unverified) treats embryos as "juridical persons" that cannot be intentionally destroyed. Patients there typically ship surplus embryos out of state. This is a key state-specific item to verify.
