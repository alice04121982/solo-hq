# US Fertility Patient Demand, Audience Segments and Go-to-Market Channels (as of September 2026)

Research notes for CairnFertility (UK IVF clinic comparison/guidance site for solo mums, solo dads, two mums, two dads and couples) on which US audiences and states to target first.

Method note: several primary sites (sart.org, asrm.org, kff.org, news.gallup.com) were blocked by the network egress proxy during this research, so some figures come from search-result summaries of those primary pages rather than full-page reads. These are flagged "(search summary of primary source)". Figures should be spot-checked before publication.

---

## 1. Demand size: how big is the US fertility patient pool?

### Takeaway
US demand is large and growing fast: about 450,000 SART-reported IVF cycles and over 100,000 IVF babies in 2024 (the first year above 100k), egg freezing growing roughly 30-40% a year, first births getting later, and 42% of adults having used fertility treatment or knowing someone who has. LGBTQ+ identification (about 9%, over 20% of Gen Z) and single-parent-by-choice demand make the non-traditional segments CairnFertility serves a large and growing share, though the US has no single authoritative count of IVF cycles for single or LGBTQ+ patients.

### Cited Findings
**Infertility prevalence (CDC NSFG; data from 2015-2019, older)**
- 16.3% of married women aged 15-49 have impaired fecundity (difficulty conceiving or carrying to term); 26.0% among those with no births. 8.5% of married women aged 15-49 are infertile (19.4% of those with no births). 13.4% of all women aged 15-49 (any marital status) have impaired fecundity. Source data: NSFG 2015-2019 — [CDC NSFG Key Statistics](https://cdc.gov/nchs/nsfg/key_statistics/i-keystat.htm); [CDC FastStats Infertility](https://cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/infertility.htm); [NHSR No. 202, April 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhsr/nhsr202.pdf)
- Note: the NSFG "infertility" definition requires 12 months of unprotected sex with a husband or cohabiting partner, so it structurally excludes single women and same-sex couples, CairnFertility's core segments — [CDC NSFG Key Statistics](https://cdc.gov/nchs/nsfg/key_statistics/i-keystat.htm)

**ART volumes (SART 2024, released 2026)**
- SART member clinics reported 449,772 IVF treatment cycles in 2024, with 97,549 cycles leading to a live birth and 100,158 babies born. This is the first year US IVF births passed 100,000. 364 clinics reported. About 79% of embryo transfers were elective single embryo transfer; more than 97% of births were singletons — (search summary of primary source) [ASRM press release](https://www.asrm.org/news-and-events/asrm-news/press-releasesbulletins/for-the-first-time-more-than-100000-babies-born-through-ivf-in-the-u.s.-in-a-single-year/); [SART National Summary 2024](https://sartcorsonline.com/Csr/PublicSnapshotReport?reportingYear=2024)
- About "1 in 36 babies" is now born through IVF (clinic blog summarising SART/CDC data; secondary source) — [Illume Fertility](https://www.illumefertility.com/fertility-blog/ivf-statistics)

**Egg freezing growth**
- SART-reported egg freezing cycles rose 39.2% year on year in 2023: over 40,000 people froze eggs in 2023 against about 29,000 in 2022. Cycles also rose about 30% from 2021 to 2022 (secondary source summarising SART data) — [Cofertility, State of Egg Freezing](https://www.cofertility.com/freeze-learn/state-of-egg-freezing-2025)
- Elective egg-freezing patients rose from 4,153 in 2014 to 16,436 in 2021, about a fourfold increase — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/elective-egg-freezing-increases-fourfold-over-7-years-2025a1000no2)

**Later first births**
- The mean age of mothers at first birth was 27.6 in 2024 (final data), up from 26.6 in 2016 and 27.5 in 2023 — [NCHS Births: Final Data for 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nvsr/nvsr75/nvsr75-02.pdf); [NCHS Trends in Mean Age of Mothers 2016-2023](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nvsr/nvsr74/nvsr74-09.pdf)

**Public familiarity**
- 42% of US adults say they have used fertility treatment or personally know someone who has, up from 33% in 2018. The figure is 48% for White adults, 45% for Asian adults, about a third of Hispanic adults and 26% of Black adults. It is 47% for women and 37% for men, and much higher among upper-income adults (Pew, 2023) — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/09/14/a-growing-share-of-americans-say-theyve-had-fertility-treatments-or-know-someone-who-has/)
- 70% of Americans say access to IVF is a good thing (Pew, 2024) — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/05/13/americans-overwhelmingly-say-access-to-ivf-is-a-good-thing/)

**Unmet need (KFF 2024 Women's Health Survey)**
- The sample was 3,901 women aged 18-49 (fieldwork May-June 2024). About 10% of reproductive-age women report having needed and obtained fertility services, and 3% needed them but were unable to get them. Among women who needed services, 24% of lower-income women gave cost as the main reason they could not get them, against 6% of higher-income women. 27% of large firms offering health benefits cover IVF (2024 KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey) — (search summary of primary source) [KFF, Access to Fertility Care](https://www.kff.org/womens-health-policy/access-to-fertility-care-findings-from-the-2024-kff-womens-health-survey/); [AJMC summary](https://x.com/AJMC_Journal/status/1849541862798880771)

**LGBTQ+ identification and family-building**
- 9.3% of US adults identified as LGBTQ+ in 2024 (Gallup), up from 3.5% in 2012. 23.1% of Gen Z adults (born 1997-2006) identify as LGBTQ+. Bisexual is the most common identity — (search summary of primary source) [Gallup 2024](https://news.gallup.com/poll/656708/lgbtq-identification-rises.aspx); [Advocate](https://www.advocate.com/news/lgbtq-population-9-percent-2025)
- Gallup's 2025 figure "holds at 9%", matching the 2024 record. Lifeway (April 2026) describes identification as flat overall but still growing among Gen Z — [Gallup 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/702206/lgbtq-identification-holds.aspx); [Lifeway Research](https://research.lifeway.com/2026/04/08/lgbtq-identification-flat-overall-still-growing-among-gen-z/)
- Family Equality's LGBTQ Family Building Survey (2018 fieldwork, published 2019; OLDER DATA; ORC International, more than 1,500 respondents) found:
  - 63% of LGBTQ+ people aged 18-35 are considering expanding their families.
  - 48% of LGBTQ+ millennials plan to have children, against 55% of non-LGBTQ+ millennials.
  - 77% of LGBTQ millennials already have children or are considering it, against 33% of LGBTQ people aged 55 and over.
  - 63% of LGBTQ+ millennials who want a family expect to use ART, foster care or adoption.
  
  — [Family Equality](https://familyequality.org/resource/lgbtq-family-building-survey/); [Survey PDF](https://familyequality.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/LGBTQ-Family-Building-Study_Jan2019-1.pdf)

**Share of IVF used by single women and LGBTQ+ patients**
- SART first collected partner sex in 2022. A SART CORS study of 2022 found 2,835 autologous IVF embryo transfer cycles (2,122 couples) and 866 reciprocal IVF transfer cycles (700 couples) among female patients with female partners — [PMC: Outcomes of Same-Sex Female Couples](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12902921/)
- 54% of Cryos sperm and egg bank customers are single women, and 78% of those single women are aged 36-45 (vendor data, not independent; global customer base, not US-only) — [Cryos International](https://www.cryosinternational.com/en-us/us-shop/client/donor-sperm/why-use-a-sperm-donor/single-mother-by-choice/)
- UK comparator: the HFEA reports female same-sex couples' IVF cycles rose from 320 (2008) to 2,151 (2018) and single patients' cycles from 531 to 1,352. Only 14% of cycles for female same-sex couples and 6% for single patients were NHS-funded, against 39% for heterosexual couples — [HFEA](https://www.hfea.gov.uk/about-us/news-and-press-releases/2020/new-figures-show-how-different-people-are-using-ivf/)
- Among the four major US cryobanks, 53% of available sperm comes from White donors and under 4% from Black donors — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/nationwide-shortage-diverse-sperm-donors-cryobank/)

### Inferences
- The 2022 SART figures give about 3,700 embryo transfers for female-female couples out of about 450k total cycles, which looks under 1%. That understates the segment: the count covers transfers only, excludes IUI (the usual first step for two mums and solo mums), and partner-sex data is new and likely incompletely recorded. A careful claim is "low single-digit % of IVF, larger share of donor-sperm IUI".
- Single women are probably a larger US IVF and IUI segment than female couples, because at cryobanks single women outnumber couples as customers. They are also the core egg-freezing market, the fastest-growing US ART service. A US "solo mum" content pillar that bridges egg freezing, donor sperm, IUI and IVF plausibly has the largest addressable audience.
- Two-dad families (gestational carrier plus egg donor) are a small-volume, very high-cost (often above US$150k), high-research-intensity segment. Comparison content could have outsized value for them, but the US surrogacy market is a separate specialist space.

### Gaps
- No verified CDC or SART US figure for the share of IVF cycles for single women (SART public tables were blocked). The CDC NASS "patient characteristics" tables should be checked directly.
- No updated (post-2019) Family Equality family-building survey was found. Other US data on solo mothers by choice (for example, trends in births to unmarried women aged 35+ using donor sperm) was not found.
- CDC's 2023 national ART report headline totals (which cover all ART, not just SART members) were not retrieved.

## 2. Where demand is concentrated: states, mandates and metros

### Takeaway
ART use is heavily concentrated in Northeast mandate states and DC (up to about 2.5x the national per-capita rate). California and Minnesota added comprehensive IVF mandates from 1 January 2026, and California's explicitly covers single and LGBTQ+ patients. Large non-mandate states (Texas, Florida and most of the South) combine big populations with high out-of-pocket costs: the "cost comparison" audience.

### Cited Findings
- In 2020 the national rate was 2,650 ART cycles per million women aged 15-49; DC's was 6,585. DC and six states (Connecticut, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York) exceeded 1.5 times the national rate. Puerto Rico was lowest at 482 per million (OLDER DATA, 2020) — [CDC 2020 State-Specific ART Surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/art/state-specific-surveillance/2020/)
- Share of babies conceived via IVF in 2022: DC 7.3%, Massachusetts 6.2%, New Jersey 5.6%, Connecticut 5%, New York 4.6% — (search summary) [USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/articles/how-many-ivf-babies-are-born-in-the-us/)
- From reporting year 2022, CDC publishes state-specific ART figures online through a dashboard covering 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico. Per-capita rates use women aged 15-49 as the denominator — [CDC ART Surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/art/php/surveillance/index.html); [CDC NASS Technical Notes](https://www.cdc.gov/art/php/nass/technical-notes.html)
- California's SB 729 took effect on 1 January 2026 (1 July 2027 for CalPERS). It covers fully insured large-group plans (101+ employees) but not small-group, self-funded or religious employers. It widens the definition of infertility to include same-sex couples and single parents by choice — [RESOLVE CA SB 729 FAQ](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/insurance-coverage/understanding-californias-ivf-insurance-law/); [CCRM](https://www.ccrmivf.com/blog/california-sb729-fertility-coverage/); [UCLA Law Center on Reproductive Health](https://law.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/PDFs/Center_on_Reproductive_Health/California%E2%80%99s%20SB%20729%20Expanding%20Access%20to%20IVF%20and%20Family-Building%20for%20All%20(1).pdf)
- According to RESOLVE, California joins 14 other states that mandate some form of IVF coverage. Minnesota's Building Families Act also took effect on 1 January 2026, and Georgia added a fertility-preservation mandate from the same date. Aggregators count 15 states plus DC requiring IVF coverage, and about 10 more mandating some fertility coverage that may exclude IVF — [RESOLVE via search summary](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/insurance-coverage/understanding-californias-ivf-insurance-law/); [ConceiveGuide 2026](https://conceiveguide.com/blog/ivf-insurance-by-state.html); [CoveredUSA 2026](https://coveredusa.org/en/qa/ivf-coverage-by-state) (aggregators, lower reliability)
- On 16 October 2025 the Trump administration announced IVF drug discounts via TrumpRx.gov (EMD Serono direct-to-patient; up to 84% off list price when all three drugs are used together; site launching January 2026). It also announced guidance letting employers offer standalone fertility benefits, similar to dental or vision. There is no mandate, subsidy or tax incentive — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/16/trump-announces-efforts-to-expand-access-to-ivf-drugs.html); [KFF analysis](https://www.kff.org/womens-health-policy/will-trumps-announcement-expand-access-to-ivf/); [Prelude, updated May 2026](https://www.preludefertility.com/blog/trumps-ivf-executive-order-what-you-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- **Mandate states (users need "how do I use my coverage" help):** high-utilisation Northeast states (MA, NJ, NY, CT, MD, IL, RI, DE and others), plus CA and MN since January 2026. The CA law is new and complicated (fully insured large-group only; self-funded ERISA plans excluded), which creates strong informational demand in 2026-27: "Does SB 729 cover me?", "SB 729 single women", "SB 729 same-sex couples".
- **High demand, low coverage (cost-comparison audience):** Texas, Florida, Georgia (preservation-only mandate), North Carolina, Arizona, Tennessee, Pennsylvania and Washington. These are large, growing populations without a comprehensive IVF mandate (per the aggregators above; verify each state against RESOLVE's state map). The TrumpRx drug discount is a live cost topic that cost-comparison content should cover.
- ERISA self-funded plans are exempt from state mandates, so even in mandate states many employees depend on employer benefits (and on vendors such as Progyny and Carrot). "Check your employer benefit" content matters nationally.

### Gaps
- The CDC 2022 and 2023 state dashboard (cycles per million by state) could not be retrieved. The report writer should use 2020 figures flagged as older, or pull the dashboard directly.
- No authoritative, verified list of all mandate states and their single or LGBTQ+ eligibility wording was retrieved (RESOLVE's state map is the canonical source). The definitions of infertility used in NY, IL, CO, NJ, etc. and whether they cover singles or same-sex couples need checking.
- No per-metro cycle data was found. Clinic density by MSA (for example from SART clinic listings) was not obtained.

## 3. Disparities: race, ethnicity, rurality, income and military

### Takeaway
Black and Hispanic women use IVF far less than White women despite equal or higher infertility. Mandates raise use overall but do not close racial gaps. Lower-income women are four times as likely to cite cost as the barrier. Military families lost a proposed TRICARE IVF benefit in the final FY2026 NDAA, so they remain an underserved, cost-sensitive niche.

### Cited Findings
- Black women aged 33-44 have twice the adjusted odds of infertility of White women, yet Black and Hispanic women are less likely to access fertility care and less likely to have a successful IVF cycle — [ACOG Committee Statement, January 2025](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-statement/articles/2025/01/infertility-disparities-and-access-to-services); [PMC: Racial disparities and IVF outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7677767/)
- State IVF mandates are not associated with narrower racial or ethnic disparities. In 2019, White minus Black utilisation was 23.5 cycles per 10,000 women in non-mandated states and 56.2 per 10,000 in mandated states — [AJOG / PubMed 36356698](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36356698/)
- Black and Hispanic women travelled a median of 10 miles for treatment, against 5 miles for White and Asian women. Hispanic women do not have higher infertility prevalence but use infertility services less — [PMC: Disparities in access to fertility care](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8244333/); [PMC: Determinants of utilization in a mandate state](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11993341)
- Among women who needed fertility services, 24% of lower-income women versus 6% of higher-income women cited cost as the main reason they could not get them — (search summary) [KFF](https://www.kff.org/womens-health-policy/access-to-fertility-care-findings-from-the-2024-kff-womens-health-survey/)
- 26% of Black adults and about a third of Hispanic adults have used fertility treatment or know someone who has, against 48% of White adults — [Pew 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/09/14/a-growing-share-of-americans-say-theyve-had-fertility-treatments-or-know-someone-who-has/)
- Under 4% of available sperm at the four major US cryobanks comes from Black donors, which matters for Black solo mothers and two-mum families — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/nationwide-shortage-diverse-sperm-donors-cryobank/)
- Military: a TRICARE IVF coverage provision passed both chambers but was removed from the final FY2026 NDAA (December 2025). TRICARE covers IVF only for service members whose infertility results from serious illness or injury on active duty. The bipartisan IVF for Military Families Act (H.R. 6797) is pending — [Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2025/12/08/2026-national-defense-authorization-act-targets-woke-ideology-cuts-ivf-military-families.html); [The 19th](https://19thnews.org/2025/12/2026-ndaa-defense-bill-military-spending/); [MilSpouses](https://milspouses.com/spouse-benefits/ndaa-ivf)

### Inferences
- Low awareness plus low exposure through personal networks among Black and Hispanic audiences means content needs to explain basics (what an REI is, how to get referred) and not only compare prices. There is a gap to fill with inclusive guidance, including donor diversity for solo mums and two-mum families.
- Military families are a clearly defined, cost-sensitive niche that relocates often and have strong online communities (MilSpouse groups). "IVF near [base]" or military-discount content could be a cheap, targeted acquisition route.

### Gaps
- No quantified rural-access data (for example the share of counties without an ART clinic) was retrieved in this pass.
- No data found on how many military families pursue IVF.

## 4. How US patients research: search, social, advocacy, referrals, employers

### Takeaway
US patients mix search, peer communities (Reddit, Facebook groups), creator content (TikTok, Instagram), advocacy groups (RESOLVE) and employer benefit vendors. Peer content on TikTok is mostly personal experience from patients rather than clinicians, which leaves room for trusted, neutral comparison. Hard search-volume numbers were not obtainable in this session.

### Cited Findings
- In an analysis of 1,905 fertility and ART TikTok posts, 66.82% were created by patients and 12.39% by physicians. Personal-experience posts made up 61.36% and educational posts 28.45% — [PubMed 41865501 / RBMO](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41865501/)
- In a small patient survey (n=23, Toronto, 2023), patients liked fertility TikTok for empathy and shared experience, stress relief and self-education, and disliked misinformation, commercialisation and negative emotions. Only 17.4% were "frequent" TikTok users for fertility content (small sample, Canadian) — [PMC12056440](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12056440/)
- A study of the five largest IVF Facebook and Reddit groups found that patients seek guidance there, while providers mainly publish on TikTok and Instagram. The authors report a mismatch between the questions patients ask and the answers provider content gives — [PMC: Lost in translation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12559448/)
- Content analysis of #IVF TikTok videos is also available — [PMC10723058](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10723058)
- Only 27% of large firms offering health benefits cover IVF (KFF 2024 EHBS). Federal guidance from October 2025 encourages standalone employer fertility benefits — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/womens-health-policy/access-to-fertility-care-findings-from-the-2024-kff-womens-health-survey/); [KFF analysis](https://www.kff.org/womens-health-policy/will-trumps-announcement-expand-access-to-ivf/)
- RESOLVE maintains the canonical state-by-state insurance and mandate explainers, including the SB 729 FAQ, so it is a natural partner and link target — [RESOLVE](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/insurance-coverage/understanding-californias-ivf-insurance-law/)

### Inferences
- Channel priorities for a comparison site:
  1. SEO on cost and coverage queries ("IVF cost [state/city]", "SB 729", "egg freezing cost [city]", "IVF for single women", "reciprocal IVF cost").
  2. Genuine, non-promotional participation in Reddit and Facebook communities.
  3. TikTok and Instagram explainers that answer the questions patients actually ask.
  4. Partnerships with RESOLVE, Family Equality, LGBTQ+ centres and single-mother-by-choice groups.
  5. Employer HR and benefits channels (B2B2C).
- Referral path language: most US patients start with an OB-GYN, who refers to an REI clinic. OB-GYN-facing content ("when to refer") is a secondary channel.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends or keyword-volume data (Ahrefs, Semrush or Google Keyword Planner) was retrieved. Monthly volumes for "IVF cost", "IVF near me" and "egg freezing cost" need a keyword tool.
- Subscriber counts for r/IVF, r/infertility, r/SingleMothersbyChoice and r/lgbtfamilies were not verified. Check them directly on Reddit.
- No US survey on podcast use by fertility patients was found.

## 5. Cultural and language differences from the UK; faith perspectives

### Takeaway
US content needs US terminology (OB-GYN, REI or "fertility doctor", "cycle", insurance and "out-of-pocket", "benefits"), Spanish-language support for a large under-served Hispanic audience, and faith content aimed at the US. The most important US faith development is the Southern Baptist Convention's June 2024 resolution opposing IVF as commonly practised. Most US Catholics nevertheless support IVF access despite Church teaching.

### Cited Findings
- In June 2024 the Southern Baptist Convention passed a non-binding resolution opposing IVF as practised. It says "not all technological means of assisting human reproduction are equally God-honoring". It urges members to use only technologies consistent with the "unconditional value and right to life of every human being, including those in an embryonic stage", and encourages adopting frozen embryos — [The 19th](https://19thnews.org/2024/06/southern-baptist-convention-ivf-women-pastors/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/saradorn/2024/06/12/southern-baptists-oppose-ivf-in-convention-vote-signaling-evangelical-shift-against-procedure/); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/southern-baptist-convention-opposition-ivf-faithful-women-options-rcna156994)
- 70% of Americans see IVF access as good; among Catholics the figure is 65%, with 27% unsure (Pew, 2024) — [Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/05/13/americans-overwhelmingly-say-access-to-ivf-is-a-good-thing/)
- 83% of US Catholics say the Church should allow couples to use IVF, although Church teaching holds IVF morally impermissible (Pew, April 2025) — [Pew Religion 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/04/30/most-us-catholics-say-they-want-the-church-to-be-more-inclusive/)
- A poll found Catholic support for IVF falls below 50% when Church teaching is explained (Catholic media; potential advocacy framing) — [Catholic Times / EWTN](https://catholictimescolumbus.org/us-world/poll-catholic-support-for-ivf-falls-below-50-when-church-teaching-explained/)
- Hispanic women do not have higher infertility prevalence but use infertility services less than non-Hispanic White women — [PMC11993341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11993341)

### Inferences
- Terminology to adapt from UK to US:
  - GP becomes OB-GYN or primary care provider.
  - "Consultant" becomes reproductive endocrinologist (REI) or "fertility doctor".
  - NHS funding or postcode lottery becomes insurance coverage, state mandate or employer benefit.
  - HFEA becomes SART and CDC for success rates. There is no licensing regulator equivalent: FDA regulates tissue, and states vary.
  - "Round" is also common in the US, but "cycle" dominates in clinical data.
  - Patients also talk in terms of prior authorisation, deductible and out-of-pocket maximum.
- Faith section: US evangelical content should cover the SBC resolution, embryo adoption and approaches that limit embryo creation. Catholic content should cover restorative reproductive medicine and NaProTechnology, which are promoted as alternatives. Jewish content should cover strong communal support for IVF, rabbinic supervision and Orthodox Jewish supervision services (strong in NY and NJ). Muslim content should cover the permissibility of IVF within marriage and restrictions on donor gametes, which bears on the solo and LGBTQ+ positioning.
- A single site serving solo and LGBTQ+ patients alongside a faith section needs careful editorial framing in the US South, where the SBC is strongest.
- The post-Dobbs "embryo personhood" context (for example the Alabama Supreme Court's February 2024 LePage ruling) affects perceived legal risk. The report writer should take sourcing for this from the legal researcher.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on Spanish-language fertility search volume or Spanish-speaking IVF patients. A keyword tool is needed.
- No US Muslim or Jewish survey data on IVF attitudes was retrieved in this pass.

## 6. Trust signals and recommended launch states

### Takeaway
US patients look for board-certified REIs, SART membership and clinic-specific success rates reported to SART and CDC, plus reviews. The recommended first markets are California, New York, Texas and Florida, with Illinois or Massachusetts as a fifth. These combine population, cycles, a mix of "use my coverage" and "compare costs" needs, and (for CA, NY, IL and MA) legal and coverage frameworks friendly to single and LGBTQ+ patients.

### Cited Findings
- SART membership and reporting: 364 clinics reported to SART for 2024. Clinic-level outcomes are public through SART CORS — [SART National Summary](https://sartcorsonline.com/Csr/PublicSnapshotReport?reportingYear=2024)
- CDC NASS publishes state and clinic ART surveillance — [CDC ART Surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/art/php/surveillance/index.html)
- California SB 729 explicitly covers single parents by choice and same-sex couples from 2026 — [RESOLVE](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/insurance-coverage/understanding-californias-ivf-insurance-law/)
- NY, IL, MA, NJ, CT, MD and DC all have ART use at or above 1.5 times the national per-capita rate (2020) — [CDC 2020](https://www.cdc.gov/art/state-specific-surveillance/2020/)

### Inferences (recommendations; not directly sourced unless cited above)
| Rank | State | Rationale | Primary content need |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **California** | Largest population; large LGBTQ+ population and a hub for surrogacy and two-dad families; brand-new SB 729 mandate covering singles and same-sex couples (Jan 2026), with confusing eligibility (large-group, fully insured only) | "Does SB 729 cover me?" plus cost comparison for those excluded (self-funded and small-group plans) |
| 2 | **New York** | High per-capita ART use; NYC is a dense clinic market; existing IVF mandate for large-group plans; large Jewish, LGBTQ+ and solo-mum populations | Coverage navigation plus clinic comparison in a crowded market |
| 3 | **Texas** | Very large, young, fast-growing population with a large Hispanic share; no comprehensive IVF mandate (verify), so high out-of-pocket pain; big metros (Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio) | Cost comparison, Spanish content, faith (evangelical and Catholic) content |
