# US Fertility Information, Comparison & Navigation Services: Competitive Landscape and Business Models (as of Sept 2026)

Research note: Several primary sources (investors.progyny.com, sec.gov, businesswire.com, fertilitybridge.com, webtonic.io) were blocked by the network proxy, so some figures below come from search-result summaries of those pages rather than full-page reads. These are marked "(search summary)". Anything unverified is flagged.

## 1. Direct consumer competitors: what they do well and badly

### Takeaway
The US consumer layer is split between (a) official but thin, lagging outcome data (SART/CDC), whose federal side was weakened when the CDC's ART team was cut in 2025; (b) one dominant independent education/review brand (FertilityIQ) that has moved toward employer-paid revenue; and (c) mission-driven nonprofits for specific pathways (Men Having Babies for gay dads, RESOLVE, etc.) that raise money through provider sponsorship and conferences. Nobody clearly combines price + success + eligibility comparison with pathway-specific guidance for solo and LGBTQ+ IVF patients. That combination is where CairnFertility could stand out.

### Cited Findings
**SART / CDC (official outcome data)**
- Clinics must report success rates to the CDC under federal law. SART members also report yearly, and that data is validated by CDC and SART audit teams — [SART: Success Rates](https://www.sart.org/patients/a-patients-guide-to-assisted-reproductive-technology/general-information/success-rates/); [RSC Bay Area explainer](https://rscbayarea.com/article/interpreting-sart-ivf-success-rates/)
- SART's "Patient Predictor" is built on more than 2 million cycles and estimates cumulative live-birth rates over up to three cycles for patients with similar characteristics — [SART Predictor press page](https://sartcorsonline.com/Predictor/PressLanding) (search summary)
- SART publishes clinic outcome tables for reporting year 2023 — [SART Outcome Tables 2023](https://www.sartcorsonline.com/EmbryoOutcome/PublicSARTOutcomeTables?reportingYear=2023&ClinicPKID=0). The CDC's final clinic-level dataset covers procedures from 2020 to 2022 — [CDC ART Success Rates](https://www.cdc.gov/art/success-rates/index.html) (search summary). The data runs about two to three years behind.
- April 2025: HHS cuts eliminated the CDC's entire six-person ART surveillance team, and some of its work was paused "indefinitely". That team built the CDC IVF Success Estimator. Clinics still have a legal duty to report, and the submission platform stayed online — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/cdcs-ivf-team-gutted-even-trump-calls-fertilization-president-rcna199261); [Healthcare Brew](https://www.healthcare-brew.com/stories/2025/04/11/hhs-layoffs-reproductive-health-research); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2025-04-21/cdc-job-cuts-eliminate-team-tracking-ivf-clinic-success-rates)
- A fertility doctor argued in STAT (June 2025) that IVF needs more regulation, in the context of the CDC ART surveillance cuts — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/06/16/ivf-regulation-cdc-hhs-art-surveillance-fertility-clinic-data-success-rates/)
- Weaknesses of SART/CDC for consumers: no prices, no eligibility or inclusivity information, and data that lags by years. (This is an inference from the sources above plus the tools' scope.)

**FertilityIQ**
- It offers online courses, lessons and reviews of fertility doctors, and has some of the best consumer-generated data in the field — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/fertilityiq); [Fertility Bridge profile, 2019](https://www.fertilitybridge.com/inside-reproductive-health/2019/5/17/b61s94q1b1k9lnmytw44x59b8nfllp) (search summary)
- The founders self-funded it and took no outside VC — [Fertility Bridge 2019 profile](https://www.fertilitybridge.com/inside-reproductive-health/2019/5/17/b61s94q1b1k9lnmytw44x59b8nfllp) (search summary)
- Business model: it sells courses to employers as an employee benefit. It reported 300% revenue growth in 2021, 100% client retention for three years running, and a 30%+ increase in courses offered to employees in 2021 — [BusinessWire, Feb 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220201005151/en/FertilityIQ-Records-300-Annual-Growth-100-Client-Retention) (search summary; data from 2022, now old)
- It runs an annual Workplace Index built from benefit policies submitted by more than 50,000 users globally. Employers are scored partly on whether they provide "non-financially-conflicted education", which is FertilityIQ's own positioning — [FertilityIQ Workplace Index](https://www.fertilityiq.com/fertilityiq/articles/fertilityiq-workplace-index)
- Its course catalogue is online — [FertilityIQ Courses](https://www.fertilityiq.com/fertilityiq/courses)

**Men Having Babies (gay dads), a nonprofit**
- It runs the "Surrogacy Advisor" directory of more than 250 providers (agencies and IVF clinics), with cost estimates, comparison tools, and ratings and reviews drawn from thousands of parent surveys — [MHB Surrogacy Advisor](https://surrogacyadvisor.menhavingbabies.org/surrogacy-directory); [MHB](https://menhavingbabies.org/)
- Revenue and funding mechanics: its conferences (NY, SF, Chicago, Miami and others; SF 2026 ran March 27–29) are sponsored by providers, and proceeds fund the Gay Parenting Assistance Program. That program gives hundreds of couples more than $1M a year in cash grants, discounts and free services from 70+ providers. A Membership Benefits Program offers discounts from 150+ providers. "Impact Partners" include networks of industry providers — [MHB](https://menhavingbabies.org/); [MHB conferences](https://www.menhavingbabies.org/surrogacy-seminars/); [MHB B2B](https://menhavingbabies.org/b2b-resources/)

**Carrot (as a content competitor)**
- Carrot publishes consumer-facing LGBTQ+-inclusive benefit content, so the employer navigators also compete for inclusive-content search traffic — [Carrot blog](https://www.get-carrot.com/blog/what-to-look-for-in-an-lgbtq-inclusive-fertility-benefit)

### Inferences
- MHB is the closest US analogue to CairnFertility's pathway-plus-community model, and it shows that provider sponsorship can coexist with credible ratings in a nonprofit setting. It covers surrogacy for gay men only. Solo mothers, lesbian/queer women using donor sperm, and trans patients have no equivalent comparison tool.
- The CDC data vacuum since 2025 creates two things for an independent site: an opening to present clinic success data clearly (SART's 2023 tables remain public), and a risk if federal data publication stops.
- FertilityIQ's shift to employer B2B suggests that paid consumer subscriptions alone were not the main revenue path, even for the category leader.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current details on CNY Fertility (low-cost, high-volume positioning), Fertility Out Loud (a Ferring-sponsored education site), Path2Parenthood, RESOLVE (nonprofit; it lists financing programs at [RESOLVE](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/financing-programs-for-fertility-treatment/)), Single Mothers by Choice, or Gay Parents To Be. I found no sourced revenue or traffic figures for them.
- I found no current (2025–26) FertilityIQ revenue, user or pricing figures, and nothing confirming whether it takes any clinic money.
- I have no sourced sizing for the Reddit (r/IVF, r/infertility) or TikTok communities.
- I could not confirm whether CDC published any new clinic-level data after April 2025. Current status needs checking.

## 2. Employer benefit navigators

### Takeaway
Employer navigators are large, and they direct employees to clinics inside curated networks. Progyny alone covered about 6.7M members across 555 clients and $1.29B in revenue in 2025. Maven (valued at $1.7B) and Carrot (1,000+ employers) are the other scaled players. For a benefited patient, the choice is "which in-network clinic?" rather than "which clinic in the country?". A US comparison site therefore needs a network/benefit filter, or it becomes irrelevant to the most valuable patient segment.

### Cited Findings
- **Progyny FY2025:** revenue $1,288.7M, up 10% (fertility benefits +14%, Progyny Rx +5%). 555 clients and about 6.7M members at 31 Dec 2025. 65,006 ART cycles in 2025, up from 61,114 in 2024. Record operating cash flow of $210.2M. About 6.5M shares repurchased. It has expanded into pregnancy/postpartum, menopause, benefit/leave navigation and parent/child wellbeing, which were not significant revenue in 2025 — [Progyny Q4/FY2025 release](https://investors.progyny.com/news-releases/news-release-details/progyny-inc-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-results); [SEC exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1551306/000155130626000012/exhibit991-annualfy25.htm) (search summary; the full page was blocked, so 2026 guidance was not captured)
- Progyny reportedly acquired Apryl, a Berlin/European egg-freezing benefit startup, in June 2024 to extend its global reach — [StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/apryl) (**single aggregator source, unverified; confirm before citing**)
- **Maven Clinic:** raised a $125M Series F at a $1.7B valuation in October 2024 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/10/08/womens-health-startup-maven-clinic-raises-at-1point7-billion-valuation.html); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/finance/maven-clinic-clinches-125m-invest-tech-value-based-care). Maven's reach is reported at 17M in 2024, rising to 23M–28M in 2025, with 2,000+ enterprise relationships and an Amazon win. The search summary's dates are internally inconsistent, so these figures are **unreliable** — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/maven-clinic/); [Getlatka, ~$268M ARR 2024](https://getlatka.com/companies/mavenclinic.com) (third-party estimates)
- **Carrot:** trusted by "more than a thousand" employers, health plans and health systems. On 4 August 2025 it added 4,200 clinic and provider contracts across 50 states. It launched the Carrot Rx "Flex Program" in October 2025. Total funding is about $114.9M through its Series C — [Carrot network expansion](https://www.get-carrot.com/blog/carrot-fertility-provider-expansion-2025); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/carrot-launches-carrot-rx-flex-program-for-us-employers-to-access-even-more-cost-savings-for-employees-302592996.html); [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/carrot-fertility). Glassdoor reviews report repeated layoff rounds in 2025 — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Employee-Review-Carrot-Fertility-E2522490-RVW84807973.htm) (anecdotal)
- **Kindbody** (clinic operator plus employer benefit): David Stern became CEO in June 2025 — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kindbody-names-david-stern-as-chief-executive-officer-to-lead-next-stage-of-growth-302477406.html). A Bloomberg investigation in September 2025 ("IVF Disrupted") alleged sales targets and pressure to push expensive add-ons — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-26/kindbody-doctors-push-expensive-ivf-procedures-add-ons). In February 2026 Kindbody announced a next-generation platform, piloting in 2026 with employer clients representing 3M+ covered lives and broad availability planned for 2027 — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kindbody-unveils-next-generation-fertility-platform-to-improve-outcomes-accelerate-affordability-and-intelligence-in-reproductive-care-302685936.html). It has raised more than $281M in total, per Crunchbase — [Kindbody press page](https://kindbody.com/press-article/despite-economic-uncertainty-fertility-startups-riding-pandemic-era-funding/)
- **Stork Club:** raised a $30M Series A, positioned around access to vetted clinics and protocols — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/health-wellness-biotech/stork-club-secures-30m-series-a-to-deliver-better-maternity-care-family-building/)
- **Policy:** on 16 October 2025 the White House announced (1) an EMD Serono deal selling fertility drugs such as Gonal-f at up to 84% off list price via TrumpRx.gov starting in 2026, and (2) guidance giving employers a new pathway to offer standalone fertility benefits, similar to dental or vision cover — [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/10/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-actions-to-lower-costs-and-expand-access-to-in-vitro-fertilization-ivf-and-high-quality-fertility-care/); [KFF](https://www.kff.org/womens-health-policy/will-trumps-announcement-expand-access-to-ivf/); [ASRM evaluation](https://www.asrm.org/advocacy-and-policy/fact-sheets-and-one-pagers/evaluating-the-trump-administrations-initiative-on-ivf/)
- Nearly 800 large organisations globally have introduced or enhanced family-building benefits, with growth now coming from retail, CPG, industrial and healthcare employers rather than tech or finance — [FertilityIQ Workplace Index](https://www.fertilityiq.com/fertilityiq/articles/fertilityiq-workplace-index)

### Inferences
- Progyny's roughly 65k ART cycles a year against 6.7M members means most members never use the benefit in a given year. The unbenefited or under-benefited majority of US patients (self-pay, small employers, single/LGBTQ+ people excluded by infertility definitions) is CairnFertility's natural market.
- Standalone fertility benefits (from the October 2025 guidance) could bring in smaller employers. That would create demand for neutral decision tools that do not belong to a navigator, which is a possible B2B angle.
- Negative press around Kindbody and PE-owned clinics strengthens the case for an independent, non-conflicted brand.

### Gaps
- Progyny's 2026 guidance and its network size (number of clinics) were not captured because the SEC and IR pages were blocked.
- I found no verified 2025–26 data for WINFertility, Gaia's employer product, or Cofertility's employer product.
- I did not find the mechanics of how navigators route patients (care-advocate referral rules, clinic quality tiers) beyond "curated network".

## 3. Financing and fintech

### Takeaway
Fertility financing is a crowded, clinic-partnered market (Future Family, CapexMD, Sunfish, Bundl, plus general lenders). The notable new model is Gaia, a UK-born "outcome-protected" fixed-price IVF plan that entered the US with VC money and a $100M debt facility. Gaia is directly relevant as a UK-to-US precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Gaia launched in London in 2022 with a $20M Series A. In January 2025 it raised $14M led by Peter Thiel's Valar Ventures to scale in the US. It later secured a $100M debt facility from Viola Credit, bringing total equity to about $37M — [Femtech Insider](https://femtechinsider.com/gaia-secures-14m-to-scale-fertility-insurance-platform-in-us/); [Femtech Insider on debt facility](https://femtechinsider.com/gaia-secures-100m-debt-facility-to-scale-outcome-protected-fertility-financing/); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/01/28/gaia-raises-14m-to-scale-fertility-insurance-platform-in-the-us/)
- Gaia offers fixed-cost IVF plans paid monthly. If the first cycle fails, Gaia covers the next one. It operates across 57 US locations in 23 clinic groups and uses ML risk models. It also publishes SEO content such as "Top Fertility Financing Programs for IVF (2026)" — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/health-wellness-biotech/ivf-startup-ai-fertility-probability-gaia/); [Gaia editorial](https://gaiafamily.com/en-us/learn/editorials/top-fertility-financing-programs-for-ivf-2026)
- Future Family offers fertility-only loans up to $50,000, distributed through clinic partnerships — [Future Family](https://www.futurefamily.com/); [SBIVF](https://sbivf.com/future-family-fertility-financing/)
- CapexMD offers fertility loans of $3,000–$50,000 through partner clinics — [CapexMD](https://www.capexmd.com/)
- Clinics list multiple lenders, including Sunfish, CapexMD, Future Family and Bundl — [Illume Fertility](https://www.illumefertility.com/pricing-and-insurance/fertility-financing-options); [Prelude/Bundl](https://www.preludefertility.com/patient-resources/financing); [Bundl](https://www.bundlfertility.com/other-financing-options)
- RESOLVE maintains a public list of financing programs — [RESOLVE](https://resolve.org/learn/financial-resources/financing-programs-for-fertility-treatment/)

### Inferences
- Lenders' main channel is clinic partnerships, so a comparison site would be a secondary, direct-to-consumer channel. Affiliate deals are plausible but not published.
- Gaia is both a potential partner (fixed-price plans fit a cost calculator) and a competitor for "IVF cost" search traffic.

### Gaps
- I found no published affiliate or referral payouts for Future Family, Sunfish, CapexMD, Gaia, LendingClub or Prosper.

## 4. Business models available to an independent US comparison site, with pricing benchmarks

### Takeaway
Clinics pay a lot for patients. Google CPCs of about $10 and $15–40 in big metros, and roughly $112 per lead, mean lead-gen or listing fees are financially viable. The trade-off is trust: FertilityIQ's "non-financially-conflicted" framing and scrutiny of PE-owned clinics make disclosed, firewalled models safer. Proven alternatives are employer B2B (FertilityIQ), provider sponsorship of events and assistance programs (MHB), and affiliate deals with consumer brands (Dandelion pays 10%).

### Cited Findings
- Average Google Ads CPC for fertility clinics is about $9.84, versus a healthcare average of $5.64. Competitive metros reach $15–25, and some keywords $15–40. Average cost per lead is about $112. Conversion rate is about 6.8% (8–12% with optimised landing pages). Clinics in competitive metros spend $5k–20k a month on Google Ads — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/fertility-ivf-google-ads-statistics); [Leadfynix](https://leadfynix.com/google-ads-for-ivf-clinics-cost-roi/); [IVF Growth, 2026](https://ivfgrowth.com/post/google-ads-vs-meta-ads-fertility-clinics-2026) (search summary; **agency marketing content, not independent benchmarks; no keyword-level "IVF cost" CPC found**)
- An IVF cycle costs about $10k–20k, which justifies high ad spend — [same agency sources](https://leadfynix.com/google-ads-for-ivf-clinics-cost-roi/). A nationwide median of about $22,000 per cycle is cited by Gaia — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/health-wellness-biotech/ivf-startup-ai-fertility-probability-gaia/)
- Affiliate example: Dandelion Fertility (egg freezing) pays affiliates 10% commission per enrolled participant, with a 30-day cookie — [Dandelion](https://www.dandelionfertility.com/become-an-affiliate)
- Legacy (sperm testing and freezing) is covered through some insurers (Aetna, Cigna, Emblem, UHC, depending on state). It publishes discounts, but I found no affiliate programme details — [Legacy](https://www.givelegacy.com/); [Legacy ways to save](https://www.givelegacy.com/ways-to-save)
- Provider-sponsored nonprofit model: MHB conferences, impact partners and provider discounts (see Q1) — [MHB B2B](https://menhavingbabies.org/b2b-resources/)
- Employer B2B education model: FertilityIQ (see Q1) — [BusinessWire 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220201005151/en/FertilityIQ-Records-300-Annual-Growth-100-Client-Retention)

### Inferences
- If CPA is about $112 per lead from Google, clinics could rationally pay about $50–150 per qualified lead from a trusted comparison site. This is an estimate, not sourced. Disclosing any clinic payments and keeping rankings separate from revenue would be essential.
- For CairnFertility, the lower-conflict mix is: employer or benefit-broker B2B licensing of the finder/calculator; affiliate deals with testing, egg-freezing and financing brands, clearly labelled and kept off clinical content; sponsorship of community events in the MHB style; and optional paid tiers (courses or concierge).

### Gaps
- I found no independent benchmark for fertility CPL or pay-per-call pricing on lead marketplaces, and no clinic-side total patient acquisition cost per IVF start.
- I have no current information on Modern Fertility (Ro) or Proov affiliate programmes.
- I found no data on WordStream's health vertical for 2025–26.

## 5. Documented gaps and unmet needs

### Takeaway
Research documents large, persistent gaps in cost barriers, heteronormative insurance definitions, racial and ethnic disparities in access, and consolidation into PE-backed chains. Each maps directly to CairnFertility features (cost calculator, eligibility filter, solo/LGBTQ+ guides, ownership transparency).

### Cited Findings
- **LGBTQ+/solo:** in a Progyny survey, 68% of LGBTQ+ respondents said heteronormative infertility definitions blocked access to care, and about 51% cited cost as a major barrier. IVF costs about $15k–20k per cycle and can exceed $30k — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/why-lgbtq-patients-are-paying-for-gaps-in-fertility-care-284907) (search summary)
- In 2023 ASRM updated its definition of infertility to include LGBTQ+ people and single individuals. California law amended its definition too — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/why-lgbtq-patients-are-paying-for-gaps-in-fertility-care-284907); [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/benefits/2023/08/lgbtq-and-single-fehb-participants-face-unnecessary-barriers-under-opms-definition-of-infertility/)
- December 2025: Aetna agreed in a national settlement to cover IVF for same-sex couples, with an estimated 2.8M LGBTQ+ members benefiting — [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/health/2025/12/aetna-lawsuit-lgbtq-ivf-fertility/) (search summary)
- **Race/ethnicity:** Black and Hispanic women were about 70% less likely than White women to receive any infertility treatment. They account for 4.0% and 7.6% of live births from infertility treatment, against 53.8% for White women. They are more likely to perceive race, weight and income as barriers, and may travel twice as far to their IVF clinic — [PMC narrative review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10889491/). Black women also have lower live-birth rates — [Reproductive Biology & Endocrinology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7677767/); see also [NWLC](https://nwlc.org/resource/the-importance-of-equitable-access-to-fertility-care-for-black-women/)
- **PE consolidation:** PE-affiliated fertility clinics grew from 4% in 2013 to about 32% of IVF clinics in 2023, and they performed more than half of US IVF cycles in 2023 (JAMA research letter, Yale/UMich, published December 2025) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12754700/); [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-12-private-equity-firms-oversee-ivf.html); [Fertility & Sterility abstract](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(25)00629-6/fulltext). Clinic acquisitions slowed in 2025 — [Fertility Bridge review 2025](https://www.fertilitybridge.com/news-articles/fertility-industry-year-in-review-2025) (search summary)
- The Center for American Progress argues PE financialisation can undermine access to reproductive care — [CAP](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/how-private-equity-and-the-financialization-of-health-services-can-undermine-access-to-sexual-and-reproductive-care/)

### Inferences
- An "ownership" field (independent, PE-backed, hospital or academic) in the clinic finder would be distinctive in the US. I found no competitor that surfaces it.
- Travel distance and rural access gaps suggest filters for telehealth monitoring, satellite monitoring sites and travel cost in the calculator.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced national survey quantifying consumer confusion about price opacity or insurance in 2025–26, and no dedicated rural-access dataset. Both need a follow-up search.

## 6. Recent entries, exits, funding (2024–2026) and UK/EU players entering the US

### Takeaway
Funding continues but is selective. Notable moves: Maven's $125M round (2024), Gaia's US push (2025), Cofertility's Series A (2025), and Kindbody's leadership reset and platform relaunch (2025–26). Among UK players, Gaia is the clearest UK-to-US entry. It entered through clinic-group partnerships plus debt-financed underwriting, with US SEO content, rather than as a pure information site. I found no evidence that Hertility has launched in the US.

### Cited Findings
- Gaia (London) entered the US: $14M in January 2025 (Valar), a $100M Viola Credit facility, and 57 US locations across 23 clinic groups — [Femtech Insider](https://femtechinsider.com/gaia-secures-14m-to-scale-fertility-insurance-platform-in-us/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/health-wellness-biotech/ivf-startup-ai-fertility-probability-gaia/)
- Cofertility (egg-sharing/freezing) closed its Series A in April 2025, led by Next Ventures and Offline Ventures (reported about $7.25M; about $16M total) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250416509158/en/Cofertility-Closes-Series-A-Funding-Led-by-Next-Ventures-and-Offline-Ventures-to-Revolutionize-Fertility-and-Family-Building); [TechNews180](https://technews180.com/funding-news/cofertility-is-raising-7-25-million-in-a-series-a-funding-round/)
- Apryl was reportedly acquired by Progyny in June 2024 (**unverified**, see Q2) — [StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/apryl)
- Hertility (London, at-home hormone testing): its 2026 Reproductive Report cites 600k+ assessments, and it launched the GYN-AI tool in October 2025. I found no evidence of a US launch — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/hertility/__IhslLpr3Zi65HWbG5DNrwGCFPYVccCXLpobmNbvvT3k); [Hertility](https://hertilityhealth.com/)
- Inito (at-home fertility testing) is expanding its test menu with AI-designed antibodies, as of December 2025 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/10/fertility-startup-inito-wants-to-use-ai-designed-antibodies-to-expand-at-home-health-tests/)
- Kindbody: new CEO (June 2025), Bloomberg investigation (September 2025), new platform (February 2026) — see Q2 sources.

### Inferences
- The UK-to-US precedent (Gaia) suggests leading with partnerships with multi-site US clinic groups and US-specific content, and securing US capital and advisers. A UK brand's HFEA-style transparency norms could be marketed as a trust advantage in the US.

### Gaps
- I did not verify "Fertility Family" or other UK/EU entrants to the US. I found no 2026 funding rounds for Carrot, Stork Club, Future Family or WINFertility, and no confirmed shutdowns or exits among consumer information sites.
